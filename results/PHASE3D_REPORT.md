## (f) 47 CFR 25.208(b) PFD limit: text, scope, and margins

Verbatim limit (47 CFR 25.208(b)(2); eCFR/govinfo), for NGSO FSS space stations in the **10.7-11.7 GHz** band, the applicable limit is the **lower** of these two branches (delta = elevation angle):

- per 4 kHz: `-150 dB(W/m^2)` for `0 deg < delta <= 5 deg`; `-150 + 0.5(delta - 5) dB(W/m^2)` for `5 < delta <= 25 deg`; `-140 dB(W/m^2)` for `25 < delta <= 90 deg`.
- per 1 MHz: `-126 dB(W/m^2)` for `0 deg < delta <= 5 deg`; `-126 + 0.5(delta - 5) dB(W/m^2)` for `5 < delta <= 25 deg`; `-116 dB(W/m^2)` for `25 < delta <= 90 deg`.

The two flat branches are equal (-140 dBW/m^2 per 4 kHz = -140 + 23.98 = -116.02 dBW/m^2 per MHz), so the 25-90 deg limit is the familiar -116 dBW/m^2/MHz used as the PFD-constant anchor.

**Scope.** 25.208(b) covers only 10.7-11.7 GHz.  The shared STARLINK/ONEWEB Ku user-downlink band is 10.7-12.7 GHz (`configs/bands.yaml`), so it **extends above 11.7 GHz where 25.208(b) does not apply**.  Above 11.7 GHz there is no general NGSO-FSS PFD limit; 47 CFR 25.208(o) covers only 12.2-12.7 GHz into an operational MVDDS receiver, and coordination for 11.7-12.2 GHz rests on the 25.261 sharing rules (band splitting / power criterion) rather than a PFD ceiling.  Our modelled coupling uses the **band-centre 11.7 GHz** and is therefore just inside the 25.208(b) band; the conclusions are anchored to that limit where it applies.

## (g) Analytic co-located model and its diagnosis

- E7 analytic vs simulated: `results/e7_analytic_20261019T171900Z_seed0/`
- E8 analytic diagnosis: `results/e8_analytic_diagnosis_20261019T171739Z_seed0/`

`theta*` (Starlink-interferer / OneWeb-victim, 0.45 m, PFD-constant at the 25.208(b) limit) = **17.0106 deg**, aperture-independent (the S.1428 1-48 deg envelope `32 - 25 log10(theta)` sets the threshold angle regardless of diameter; hand-checkable in `tests/test_analytic.py`).

**Diagnosis (24 h, one phasing, per latitude; the simulation is the reference).**  The two serving directions are independent (distinct constellations, independent phasing): feeding the *empirical* serving-radius marginals through the independent-azimuth separation integral reproduces the simulated conditional separation probability to within 0.025.

The original marginal (nearest-of-n directions uniform in the *look-direction* cap) is the wrong assumption: it predicts a mean serving radius of 17.9 deg, whereas the measured mean is 24.0 deg (OneWeb) / 18.2 deg (Starlink).  It therefore **over-predicts** the simulated conditional separation probability by up to 0.249 and sits above the simulation at every latitude (yes).

Replacing it with a physically justified marginal - the nearest-of-n serving satellite is uniform in the *ground footprint* and mapped to the look angle through the exact spherical elevation relation (`serving_look_radius_pdf`) - reduces the conditional error to 0.064 and removes the systematic bias (above the simulation at every latitude: no; event-fraction offset -0.064 to +0.046).  The residual is bounded by the empirical-marginal independence limit above, i.e. by the single-shell uniformity approximation, not by the independence assumption.  The full event-fraction model is therefore reported as indicative only; the simulation remains the primary result.
