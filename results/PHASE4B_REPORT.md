## 6. Basis of the as-deployed intervals and recorded limitations

- **CI basis.** 50 of the 50 non-constant daily series pass the lag-1 and trend screen and 0 fail it, so the t-interval is kept.  The interval is a 95% t-interval over consecutive days and describes day-to-day (temporal) variability of one TLE snapshot propagated forward, conditional on that snapshot and on the propagation model; it is not a sampling interval over constellation states or snapshots.  With 8 (Gen2) or 4 (Gen1-only, all-Starlink) days the screen has little power, so the independence it supports is weak.  The re-run removes the window overlap; it is not a complete fix of CI validity.

- **Propagation.** Against SGP4 on the same TLEs, the J2 secular model's median Starlink position error grows from 8.3 km (day 0) to 391.1 km (day 8), a median look-angle error of 16.5 deg; OneWeb stays near 13.8 km.  Individual Starlink positions in the later days are therefore not a forecast of the real constellation (SGP4 itself omits manoeuvres): those days act as further phasings of the observed shells.  The statistics the results use are stable: mean visible Gen2 satellites 26.49-26.51 (J2) and 25.97-26.04 (SGP4) over the days, J2 above SGP4 by 1.7-2.0%, and the geometric in-line proxy 1.02-1.13% (J2) vs 1.00-1.10% (SGP4); trend tests in Sec. 2.
