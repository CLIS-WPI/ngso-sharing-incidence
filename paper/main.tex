\begin{abstract}
Under 47 CFR~25.261, co-frequency non-geostationary (NGSO) systems must share
spectrum, and \S\,25.261(c) obliges operators to split the band when in-line
interference cannot otherwise be resolved. How often does that obligation
actually bind? We build a batched two-body-plus-$J_2$ propagation model of
Starlink, OneWeb, and Kuiper, screen every co-frequency user-downlink pair over a
global latitude grid, and apply the \S\,25.261(d) $\Delta T/T$ criterion with
ITU-R antenna patterns and a link budget. Only Starlink and OneWeb share a
co-frequency user downlink (Ku, \SharedFreqGhz~GHz). Modelled co-located in-line
events peak near \EventColocMax\ of the time; unlimited satellite diversity
resolves essentially all of them, so the band-split burden is
\BandSplitDiv\ with diversity against \BandSplitNoDiv\ without. A real CelesTrak
snapshot preserves the ordering. We identify which conclusions depend on assumed
parameters.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{T}{he} rapid growth of low-Earth-orbit (LEO) broadband
constellations has made co-frequency sharing between NGSO systems a first-order
regulatory problem. In the United States, 47 CFR~25.261 governs NGSO-NGSO
sharing~\cite{cfr25261,fcc2329}: operators must protect one another against in-line interference, and
\S\,25.261(c) requires that a portion of the assigned spectrum be shared
(\emph{band splitting}) whenever interference cannot be resolved by other means.
Whether this obligation is a rare corner case or a routine condition determines
how much capacity sharing actually costs. This letter measures the
\emph{geographic incidence} of in-line events and the resulting burden along the
operator's Ku-band user links.

\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item We assemble an open, traceable multi-constellation model (Starlink, OneWeb,
      Kuiper) and screen \emph{only} co-frequency pairs, showing that the
      user-downlink overlap reduces to a single pair, Starlink-OneWeb at
      \SharedFreqGhz~GHz (Kuiper's user downlink is Ka).
\item We quantify how often in-line events occur versus latitude and show that
      satellite diversity resolves essentially all co-located events, so the
      \S\,25.261(c) band-split burden is near zero with diversity and small
      without it.
\item We apply the \S\,25.261(d) $\Delta T/T$ criterion with a link budget,
      and repeat the analysis on a real as-deployed TLE snapshot; we report the
      direction asymmetry and the parameter sensitivities explicitly.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Model and Method}

\subsection{Constellations and propagation}
We model Starlink, OneWeb, and Kuiper as Walker-Delta shells taken from the
FCC-derived literature and filings, and propagate every satellite with a
circular-orbit two-body model plus $J_2$ secular drift of the right ascension of
the ascending node and the argument of latitude. A batched GPU implementation
keeps the full constellation in one tensor. The analytic propagator is validated
against SGP4 (residual $\approx\!15.7$~km/day). Earth is a sphere of radius
$R=6371$~km (assumed); the epoch is 2025-01-01T00:00:00Z. The unit of
replication is an independent constellation phasing (modelled cases) or an
independent simulation start time (as-deployed case), with
\EventNPhasings\ and \NStartTimes\ realizations respectively.

\subsection{Co-frequency pairs and the interference screen}
Bands are read from a configuration file that records a source for every entry.
Screening the ordered pair list for a \emph{user-downlink} overlap leaves a
single co-frequency pair, Starlink and OneWeb, sharing Ku spectrum with a
centre frequency of \SharedFreqGhz~GHz; Kuiper's user downlink is Ka and does
not overlap. Stations are placed on a grid of latitudes from $0^\circ$ to
$65^\circ$ (step $5^\circ$) on four meridians, with a $25^\circ$ minimum
elevation. An \emph{in-line event} is declared when the angular separation
between the victim's serving satellite and an interfering satellite at the
victim antenna falls below a screening half-angle $\theta\in\{1^\circ,2^\circ,5^\circ\}$.
We distinguish a \emph{co-located} variant (the potential interferer is the
interferer's own serving satellite) from a \emph{worst-case} upper bound (any
visible interferer at peak EIRP density).

\subsection{Regulatory criteria}
The power criterion uses the \S\,25.261(d) short-term limit
$\Delta T/T > \DttThreshold$ (equivalently $I/N > -12.2$~dB) and the long-term
reference $\DttLongTerm$ with an exceedance fraction of \LongTermFraction, in
the spirit of the ITU-R S.1503 methodology~\cite{itu1503}. The
victim receive gain uses the ITU-R S.1428 satellite pattern~\cite{itu1428} and
the S.465 earth station pattern~\cite{itu465,itu1528}; the link budget applies a
3~dB implementation margin.
Satellite diversity is modelled as the victim switching to any other visible
satellite of the \emph{same} system.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Geographic incidence of in-line events}
Fig.~\ref{fig:event} shows the modelled event time-fraction versus latitude for
the single co-frequency pair. The co-located (main) estimate peaks at
\EventColocMax\ of the time near \EventColocLat$^\circ$; the worst-case upper
bound reaches \EventWorstMax\ with the same latitude shape. The two ordered
directions coincide for the co-located variant, because the serving-to-serving
separation does not depend on which system is labelled the victim.

\subsection{Diversity and the \S\,25.261(c) band-split burden}
Under unlimited satellite diversity the unresolvable time-fraction is zero at
every latitude and every $\theta$ tested, so the band-split loss
($\tfrac12$ of the unresolvable fraction) is \BandSplitDiv\ with diversity
against \BandSplitNoDiv\ without. A capacity-limited variant, in which a cell
may be served by at most $N_{\text{beam}}$ beams, produces only a tiny nonzero
unresolvable cell-time fraction (maximum \CapUnresMax); the conditional
fraction per served cell-time falls monotonically with $N_{\text{beam}}$
(\CapUnresCondNbeamEight, \CapUnresCondNbeamSixteen, \CapUnresCondNbeamThirtyTwo\ for
$N_{\text{beam}}=8,16,32$), confirming that the residual burden is a capacity
effect rather than a geometric one.

\subsection{The \S\,25.261(d) \texorpdfstring{$\Delta T/T$}{Delta T/T} criterion}
Fig.~\ref{fig:dtt} applies the power criterion. The worst-case exceedance of
$\Delta T/T>\DttThreshold$ reaches \DttEventMax\ (\DttEventLat$^\circ$,
\DttEventDiam~m aperture), and the mean $\Delta T/T$ reaches \DttMeanMax,
above the \DttLongTerm\ long-term reference. The direction asymmetry is
decisive: only the OneWeb-victim\,$\leftarrow$\,Starlink-interferer direction
exceeds the threshold with the shipped EIRP densities; the reverse direction is
negligible. Time-weighted throughput degradation is at most \DegradeNoDivMax\
without diversity and \DegradeDivMax\ with unlimited diversity.

\subsection{As-deployed snapshot}
To remove idealization, we replace the Walker pattern and random phasing with a
real CelesTrak snapshot~\cite{celestrak}: \NStarlinkRaw\ Starlink and \NOnewebRaw\ OneWeb objects,
filtered to their operating altitude windows
(\NStarlinkOp\ and \NOnewebOp\ operational satellites). Elements are advanced to
a common epoch with the same $J_2$ model and run from \NStartTimes\ independent
start times. Fig.~\ref{fig:dep} shows the resulting incidence and
Fig.~\ref{fig:depvs} the comparison with the modelled Walker case. The
as-deployed worst-case fraction reaches \DepEventWorstMax\ and the power-criterion
exceedance \DepDttEventMax, while the ordering of directions and the
zero-diversity-resolved burden are preserved.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The headline result does not rest on every assumed parameter. The dominant
OneWeb-victim direction is invariant to the assumed OneWeb downlink EIRP
density: sweeping it over \EirpSweepValues~dBW/MHz leaves the forward exceedance
unchanged at \EirpFwdMax, while the reverse direction grows from \EirpRevAssumed\
to only \EirpRevEqual\ even when OneWeb is given the (larger) Starlink value.
The direction asymmetry is therefore geometric and power-driven, not an artefact
of the assumed value. Remaining assumed quantities include satellite beamwidths,
all system noise temperatures, every Walker phasing factor, the capacity and
geometry parameters of the capacity-limited case, and the operating altitude
windows used for filtering. Event fractions are sampling-limited, and the
two-body-plus-$J_2$ propagator is an approximation to SGP4. Extended filings
(for example the in-line-event claims made by operators) are noted in the
bibliography but no unverified figures are quoted here.

\section{Conclusion}
For the one co-frequency Ku user-downlink pair among Starlink, OneWeb, and
Kuiper, in-line events are a sub-percent-of-time phenomenon that satellite
diversity resolves almost completely; the \S\,25.261(c) band-splitting
obligation therefore binds only weakly, and the residual \S\,25.261(d) burden is
concentrated in a worst-case direction that depends on the interferer's power,
not on the assumed victim parameters. All numbers are generated from the
project's result files.
