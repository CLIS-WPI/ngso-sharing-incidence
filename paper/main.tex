\title{How Often Does the FCC's NGSO Band-Splitting Rule Actually Bind?\\
A Multi-Constellation Analysis of Ku-Band User Links}

% Double-anonymous submission: no author or affiliation information.
\author{Anonymous Submission}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Under 47 CFR~25.261(c), absent coordination, the spectrum of co-frequency
non-geostationary (NGSO) systems must be divided whenever the noise-temperature
increment $\Delta T/T$ exceeds 6\%---the value that defines the in-line event
that triggers band splitting. How often, and where, does this trigger fire? We
build a batched two-body-plus-$J_2$ model of Starlink, OneWeb, and Kuiper, screen
every co-frequency user-downlink pair over a global latitude grid, and evaluate
$\Delta T/T$ with a link budget. Only Starlink and OneWeb share a co-frequency
user downlink (Ku, \SharedFreqGhz~GHz). The trigger fires in the OneWeb-victim
direction a peak of \BindFilColoPct\% of the time co-located and \BindFilWorstPct\%
under a worst-case bound (as-filed); unlimited satellite diversity drives it to
zero. The \S\,25.261(d) limits on degraded throughput and link availability are
not exceeded in the direction where that rule applies. A real CelesTrak snapshot
preserves the ordering, and we mark every assumed parameter.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
NGSO, spectrum sharing, in-line interference, 47 CFR 25.261, band splitting,
Starlink, OneWeb, Kuiper.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{T}{he} rapid growth of low-Earth-orbit (LEO) broadband
constellations has made co-frequency sharing between NGSO systems a first-order
regulatory problem. In the United States, 47 CFR~25.261 governs NGSO-NGSO
sharing~\cite{cfr25261,fcc2329}. Under \S\,25.261(c), whenever the
noise-temperature increment $\Delta T/T$ at a receiver exceeds 6\% because of
another system's emissions in a commonly authorized band, the band is divided
among the affected networks and each may use only its share \emph{while the
threshold is exceeded}. The 6\% value therefore \emph{defines} the in-line event
that triggers band splitting, and how often it is exceeded determines how much
capacity sharing actually costs. This letter measures the geographic incidence of
that trigger along the operator's Ku-band user links.

\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item We build an open, traceable multi-constellation model (Starlink, OneWeb,
      Kuiper) and screen \emph{only} co-frequency pairs, showing that the
      user-downlink overlap reduces to a single pair, Starlink--OneWeb at
      \SharedFreqGhz~GHz (Kuiper's user downlink is Ka).
\item We report the fraction of time the \S\,25.261(c) 6\% $\Delta T/T$ trigger is
      exceeded versus latitude, for a co-located and a worst-case bound and for
      both the filed (Walker) and a real as-deployed geometry, and show that
      unlimited satellite diversity reduces it to zero.
\item We apply the \S\,25.261(d) degraded-throughput limits, report the direction
      asymmetry, and quantify the sensitivity of the headline result to the
      interferer's power, the terminal aperture, and the minimum elevation.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Model and Method}

\subsection{Constellations and propagation}
We model Starlink, OneWeb, and Kuiper as Walker-Delta shells taken from
FCC-derived literature and filings, and propagate every satellite with a
circular-orbit two-body model plus $J_2$ secular drift of the right ascension of
the ascending node and the argument of latitude. A batched GPU implementation
keeps the full constellation in one tensor. The analytic propagator is validated
against SGP4 (residual $\approx\!15.7$~km/day). Earth is a sphere of radius
$R=6371$~km (assumed); the epoch is 2025-01-01T00:00:00Z. The unit of
replication is an independent constellation phasing (modelled cases) or an
independent simulation start time (as-deployed case), with \EventNPhasings\
independent phasings and \NStartTimes\ independent start times respectively.

\subsection{Co-frequency pairs and the interference screen}
Bands are read from a configuration file that records a source for every entry.
Screening the ordered pair list for a \emph{user-downlink} overlap leaves a
single co-frequency pair, Starlink and OneWeb, sharing Ku spectrum with a centre
frequency of \SharedFreqGhz~GHz; Kuiper's user downlink is Ka and does not
overlap. Stations are placed on a grid of latitudes from $0^\circ$ to
$65^\circ$ (step $5^\circ$) on four meridians, with a $25^\circ$ minimum
elevation. We distinguish a \emph{co-located} variant (the potential interferer
is the interferer's own serving satellite) from a \emph{worst-case} upper bound
(any visible interferer at peak EIRP density). A geometric proxy additionally
flags an event whenever the angular separation between the victim's serving
satellite and an interfering satellite falls below a half-angle
$\theta\in\{1^\circ,2^\circ,5^\circ\}$; it is reported only as a sensitivity
comparison.

\subsection{Regulatory criteria}
Section~25.261(c) is the primary criterion: the band split is triggered while the
victim link's $\Delta T/T$ exceeds \DttThreshold\ (equivalently $I/N>-12.2$~dB),
where $I$ aggregates the interfering emissions into the victim receiver and
$N=kT_{\text{sys}}B$~\cite{cfr25261,fcc2329}. Section~25.261(d) separately
requires a later-round system to show, via a degraded-throughput methodology, no
more than \DegradeLimitPct\% time-weighted average degraded throughput and no more
than \UnavailLimitPct\% absolute change in link availability, for links with at
least 99\% baseline availability at a C/N of 0~dB~\cite{fcc24117}. We compute
throughput from a Shannon capacity with a 3~dB implementation margin and treat
C/N${}=0$~dB as the availability threshold. The victim receive gain uses the
ITU-R S.465 earth-station reference pattern~\cite{itu465} (the code's S.1428
option reduces to this envelope outside the main lobe~\cite{itu1428}); the
interferer is evaluated at peak EIRP density toward the victim, so the ITU-R
S.1528 satellite antenna pattern~\cite{itu1528} is \emph{not} applied---a
conservative choice. Satellite diversity lets the victim switch to any other
visible satellite of the \emph{same} system.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Binding of the \texorpdfstring{\S\,25.261(c)}{25.261(c)} trigger}
Fig.~\ref{fig:binding} is the headline result: the fraction of time the 6\%
$\Delta T/T$ trigger is exceeded, versus latitude, for the single co-frequency
pair. The trigger is direction-asymmetric. In the OneWeb-victim direction it
peaks at \BindFilColoPct\% of the time co-located and \BindFilWorstPct\% under the
worst-case bound (as-filed Walker geometry), both near
\BindFilWorstLat$^\circ$; the reverse, Starlink-victim direction is negligible
with the shipped power densities. The as-deployed snapshot raises the worst-case
peak to \BindDepWorstPct\% (\BindDepWorstLat$^\circ$) and the co-located peak to
\BindDepColoPct\% (\BindDepColoLat$^\circ$), because the deployed geometry packs
more interfering satellites into the same shell. The two ordered directions
coincide for the purely geometric co-located variant, since the
serving-to-serving separation does not depend on the victim label.

\subsection{As-deployed versus filed geometry}
Replacing the Walker pattern with a real CelesTrak
snapshot~\cite{celestrak} (\NStarlinkRaw\ Starlink and \NOnewebRaw\ OneWeb objects,
filtered to \NStarlinkOp\ and \NOnewebOp\ operational satellites) changes the
magnitude but not the ordering. The worst-case trigger fraction is
\BindRatioEquator$\times$ larger at the equator and \BindRatioPeak$\times$ larger
at its peak latitude than the filed model. The geometric proxy shows a milder
\GeomRatioEquator$\times$ equatorial inflation, confirming that the growth is
driven by the real along-track spacing rather than by a change of regime.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{\S\,25.261(d)}{25.261(d)} degraded-throughput limits}
Fig.~\ref{fig:throughput} reports the time-weighted throughput degradation with
no diversity, per aperture, and the 3\% limit. With unlimited diversity it is
\DegrWithDivPct\% for every aperture. Without diversity the worst-case value
reaches \DegrDepWorstPctFourFive\%, \DegrDepWorstPctSixZero\%, and
\DegrDepWorstPctSevenFive\% for the \EirpHeadDiam, 0.6, and 0.75~m terminals
(as-deployed) and \DegrFilWorstPctFourFive\%, \DegrFilWorstPctSixZero\%,
\DegrFilWorstPctSevenFive\% as-filed, so the 3\% limit is exceeded for the
smallest apertures in the worst case. This exceedance, however, occurs entirely
in the OneWeb-victim direction, whose modelled baseline availability is
\BaselineAvailOnePct\% at the assumed OneWeb downlink EIRP and therefore lies
outside the $\geq 99\%$ baseline-availability domain in which \S\,25.261(d) is
defined. In the Starlink-victim direction, where the baseline availability is at
least \BaselineAvailStarMinPct\%, degradation is $\leq\!\DegrDepStarPct\%$ and no
case approaches 3\%. The absolute link-availability change is \UnavailIncPct\% in
all cases, below the \UnavailLimitPct\% limit.

\subsection{Diversity and the residual burden}
Under unlimited satellite diversity the unresolvable time-fraction is
\BindWithDivPct\% at every latitude for both criteria, so the band-split burden
vanishes whenever the victim can select another satellite. A capacity-limited
variant, in which a cell may be served by at most $N_{\text{beam}}$ beams, is the
only case with a nonzero residue: the conditional unresolvable fraction per
served cell-time falls monotonically with $N_{\text{beam}}$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}), from \CapUnresCondNbeamEight\ at $N_{\text{beam}}=8$ to
\CapUnresCondNbeamThirtyTwo\ at $N_{\text{beam}}=32$ at $60^\circ$, and the
absolute cell-time residue never exceeds \CapUnresMaxPct\%. The residual burden is
thus a capacity effect, not a geometric one.

\section{Related Work}
Prior work quantifies NGSO coexistence by simulation rather than by the FCC's own
trigger. Braun et al.~\cite{braun2019} study Ku-band inter-system interference
across proposed NGSO and GSO systems and find that band-splitting markedly
degrades throughput. Kim, Roberts, and Andrews~\cite{kim2025} analyze in-band
Starlink--Kuiper coexistence at 20~GHz and show that a secondary system can
protect an incumbent by strategic satellite selection. This letter complements
both by centring on the \S\,25.261(c) $\Delta T/T$ trigger, reporting its
latitude-resolved incidence for both directions of a real co-frequency Ku
downlink pair, and separating the filed from the as-deployed geometry. Recent
FCC orders~\cite{fcc2329,fcc24117} define the degraded-throughput metric we
evaluate; claims by operators about observed in-line events are noted in the
bibliography but no unverified figures are quoted here.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The headline result rests on several assumptions. (a) We associate each station
with its highest-elevation satellite; operator selection policies differ and
could shift the incidence. (b) Consumer user terminals are electronically steered
flat panels, so the ITU-R S.465/S.1428 dish patterns are approximations. (c) We
model the user downlink only and omit gateways and feeder links. (d) Parameters
tagged \textsc{assumed} in \texttt{configs/}---satellite beamwidths, all system
noise temperatures, every Walker phasing factor, the terminal aperture, the
minimum elevation and the operating-altitude windows---are placeholders. The
OneWeb-victim baseline availability is additionally degenerate at the assumed
OneWeb EIRP, which is why its throughput figure is reported only for
completeness. Event fractions are sampling-limited; the two-body-plus-$J_2$
propagator is an approximation to SGP4; and the diversity result assumes perfect
knowledge of the interfering system's serving satellite.

\section{Conclusion}
For the one co-frequency Ku user-downlink pair among Starlink, OneWeb, and
Kuiper, the \S\,25.261(c) 6\% $\Delta T/T$ trigger fires in the OneWeb-victim
direction a peak of \BindFilColoPct\% of the time co-located and \BindFilWorstPct\%
under a worst-case bound as-filed (\BindDepWorstPct\% as-deployed), but satellite
diversity reduces it to \BindWithDivPct\%. The \S\,25.261(d) 3\% throughput and
\UnavailLimitPct\% availability limits are not exceeded in the direction where
that rule applies. The obligation therefore binds only in a narrow, worst-case
OneWeb-victim configuration, and not at all once diversity is available. All
numbers are generated from the project's result files.
