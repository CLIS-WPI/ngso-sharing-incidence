\title{Frequent In-Line Events, Conditional Compliance:\\
Satellite Diversity Decides the FCC's NGSO Sharing Limits}

% Double-anonymous submission: no author or affiliation information.
\author{Anonymous Submission}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Under 47 CFR~25.261, co-frequency non-geostationary (NGSO) systems split the band
while a receiver's noise-temperature increment $\Delta T/T$ exceeds
\DttThresholdPct\%, and \S\,25.261(d) limits the throughput degradation
(\DegradeLimitPct\%) and availability change (\UnavailLimitPct\%) that a
later-round system may cause an earlier-round one. We evaluate both criteria
versus latitude for the only co-frequency Ku user-downlink pair among Starlink,
OneWeb, and Kuiper, with OneWeb as the protected earlier-round victim of
Starlink Gen2, in filed and as-deployed geometry. In-line events are frequent:
the OneWeb-victim time fraction peaks at \BindDepWorstPct$\pm$\BindDepWorstCi\%
as deployed, \BurdenRatioDep$\times$ the reverse direction. Without satellite
diversity, \S\,25.261(d) is exceeded at Starlink's Gen2 filed maximum, the
25.208(b) PFD limit (\DegrDepMaxPct$\pm$\DegrDepMaxCi\% and
\UnavailIncDepPct$\pm$\UnavailIncDepCi\%), but not at its Gen1 design PFD,
\PfdOffsetGenOneDb~dB lower, where the throughput limit is met and the
availability change is not distinguishable from its limit. Satellite diversity
restores compliance in every case only when the victim can hand off to
lower-elevation satellites (a \StationMinElevDeg$^\circ$ mask); under OneWeb's
filed \OnewebMinElevSensDeg$^\circ$ design elevation the diversity pool is too
small and the availability limit is exceeded even with diversity. Compliance
therefore depends on satellite diversity and the victim's elevation mask, neither
of which the rule mentions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{o-frequency} sharing between low-Earth-orbit broadband
constellations is governed in the United States by 47 CFR~25.261~\cite{cfr25261}.
Absent coordination, \S\,25.261(c) divides the commonly authorized band among
systems of the same processing round whenever interference from the other system
raises a receiver's $\Delta T/T$ above \DttThresholdPct\%, for as long as the
excess lasts~\cite{fcc2329}; this threshold \emph{defines} the in-line event.
Between rounds, \S\,25.261(d) protects earlier-round systems: a later-round
system may cause no more than \DegradeLimitPct\% time-weighted average degraded
throughput and no more than \UnavailLimitPct\% absolute change in availability,
for links with at least \RuleBaselineAvailPct\% baseline availability at a C/N
threshold of \CnGateDb~dB~\cite{fcc24117}. The protection sunsets ten years after
the first grant in the later round, after which \S\,25.261(c) applies between the
two rounds~\cite{fcc24117}. OneWeb~\cite{fcc1777} and Starlink
Gen1~\cite{fcc1838} belong to the 2016 processing round and Starlink
Gen2~\cite{fcc2291} to the 2020 round, so \S\,25.261(d) applies to the Gen2
system protecting OneWeb, and \S\,25.261(c) between OneWeb and Gen1. A recent,
separate order replaces the equivalent-PFD framework for NGSO--GSO
sharing~\cite{fcc2626}; it does not change these NGSO--NGSO criteria.

In-line interference between the OneWeb and SpaceX user downlinks was anticipated
in~\cite[Sec.~5.1]{delportillo2019}; prior work asked whether interference among
dense NGSO constellations is a practical concern~\cite{braun2019} and analyzed
the feasibility of in-band coexistence of dense LEO systems~\cite{kim2025}.
Neither evaluates the FCC's criteria. This letter resolves the \S\,25.261(c) event
and the \S\,25.261(d) limits by latitude for this pair, with and without
satellite diversity, and shows that compliance hinges on diversity, which the
rule does not mention.

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:model}

\emph{Geometry.} Starlink, OneWeb, and Kuiper are modelled as Walker-Delta
shells propagated with a two-body model plus $J_2$ secular drift, over
\EventNPhasings\ independent constellation phasings (\emph{as filed}). The
\emph{as-deployed} geometry is a CelesTrak snapshot~\cite{celestrak}
(\NStarlinkRaw\ Starlink and \NOnewebRaw\ OneWeb objects, filtered to
\NStarlinkOp\ and \NOnewebOp\ at operational altitude) over \NStartTimes\
independent start times. The \NStations\ earth stations lie at latitudes
\LatMinDeg--\LatMaxDeg$^\circ$ in \LatStepDeg$^\circ$ steps on \NLongitudes\
meridians; each station uses the highest-elevation visible satellite of its
system. Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the parameters and their sources.

\emph{Co-frequency overlap.} Only pairs sharing spectrum on the same link type are
screened. In the user downlink this leaves Starlink and OneWeb in
\SharedBandLoGhz--\SharedBandHiGhz~GHz, coupled at \SharedFreqGhz~GHz; Kuiper's
user downlink is in Ka band.

\emph{Criterion.} An event occurs while $\Delta T/T = I/N > \DttThreshold$
($I/N > \InThresholdDb$~dB), with $N = kT_{\text{sys}}B$ and $I$ the aggregate
interference~\cite{cfr25261}. The victim gain follows ITU-R
S.1428~\cite{itu1428} and interferer off-axis emission ITU-R S.1528~\cite{itu1528}.
Three aggregates are computed. \emph{Realistic} (main result): the interfering
system's serving satellite points its main beam at the site and every other
visible interfering satellite contributes through its S.1528 sidelobe.
\emph{Co-located}: the serving satellite alone. \emph{Upper bound}: every
visible interfering satellite at peak EIRP, used only as a bound.

\emph{Interferer power.} Starlink Ku holds a constant surface PFD while
steering~\cite{fcc2291}. Two sourced anchors are used, both at the
\PfdAnchorElevDeg$^\circ$ service edge. (a) \emph{Gen2 filed maximum} (primary):
the 47 CFR~25.208(b) limit, \PfdLimitDbw~dBW/m$^2$/MHz~\cite{cfr25208} (model
\PrimaryPfdDbw); the top of the filed Gen2 EIRP range~\cite{fcc2291},
\StarlinkFiledTopDbw~dBW/MHz after conversion from the per-4-kHz unit, gives
\FiledPfdDbw, within \FiledPfdOffsetDb~dB. (b) \emph{Gen1 design point}: the
constant PFD of the 2016 filing~\cite[Table~A.7-1]{spacex2016},
\GenOnePfdDbw~dBW/m$^2$/MHz, \PfdOffsetGenOneDb~dB below the limit; for the Gen1
system itself \S\,25.261(c), not (d), applies, so (b) serves as a sourced lower
operating point of the interferer. Section~25.208(b) covers only
\PfdScopeLoGhz--\PfdScopeHiGhz~GHz of the shared band. OneWeb is constant-EIRP.

\emph{25.261(d) metrics and diversity.} Throughput is the Shannon rate with a
\ImplMarginDb~dB implementation margin; availability is the fraction of time with
C/N~$\ge \CnGateDb$~dB, an unserved instant counting as unavailable; only links
with baseline availability of at least \RuleBaselineAvailPct\% qualify. Both
metrics are invariant to the channel bandwidth ($I/N$ and C/N are per-MHz
densities and degradation is a rate ratio); replacing it by
\BwCheckMhz~MHz~\cite[Table~3]{delportillo2019} leaves every metric unchanged
(largest difference \BwMaxAbsDiffPct~pp). With satellite diversity the victim may switch
to any other visible satellite of its own system; diversity is unlimited.
\S\,25.261(d) is evaluated only for the OneWeb victim, where it legally applies.
\emph{Aggregation.} Every \S\,25.261(d) number is the worst qualifying station per
replication (phasing or start time), averaged across replications, with a
\CiPct\% $t$-interval; a limit is \emph{exceeded} only if the whole interval lies
above it and \emph{met} only if it lies below.

\section{Analytic Approximation}
\label{sec:analytic}
For a PFD-constant interferer, $I/N$ depends only on the victim's off-axis angle
$\theta$, so the event reduces to $\theta < \theta^{*}$ with
$G_{\text{rx}}(\theta^{*}) = I/N|_{\text{thr}} + N - \text{PFD} -
10\log_{10}(\lambda^2/4\pi)$. In the S.1428 envelope $G = 32 - 25\log_{10}\theta$
($1^\circ$--$48^\circ$) the aperture cancels, giving
$\theta^{*} = \AnalyticThetaStarDeg^\circ$ for the OneWeb victim (spread
\AnalyticThetaSpreadDeg$^\circ$ across \EirpHeadDiam--\AperMaxM~m). The
co-located event probability is $P = v_A v_B\,\Pr\{\angle(\hat u_A,\hat u_B) <
\theta^{*}\}$, where $v = 1 - e^{-n}$ is the probability that a system has a
satellite in view ($n$ the mean visible count) and each serving direction is the
nearest of $n$ satellites uniform in the ground footprint, mapped to the look
angle. The two serving directions are independent: the simulated marginals
reproduce the simulated separation probability within \DiagnosisIndepMaxErrPct\
percentage points (pp), whereas a marginal uniform in the look-angle cap errs by
up to \DiagnosisOldMaxErrPct~pp. Against the simulated co-located as-filed curve
the model is within \AnalyticMaxAbsErrPct~pp (signed error \AnalyticSignedMinPct\
to \AnalyticSignedMaxPct~pp); the error varies with latitude, with
under-prediction at \AnalyticUnderLatLo--\AnalyticUnderLatHi$^\circ$ and
over-prediction at \AnalyticOverLowLatLo--\AnalyticOverLowLatHi$^\circ$ and
\AnalyticOverHighLatLo--\AnalyticOverHighLatHi$^\circ$. The simulation remains
the primary result.

\emph{OneWeb's design elevation.} OneWeb's filed design keeps a satellite above
\OnewebMinElevSensDeg$^\circ$ for its users~\cite[Sec.~2.2]{delportillo2019}.
Applying this mask to the victim's serving selection, baseline, and diversity
pool in the as-deployed geometry, the time with no OneWeb satellite above it is
\MinElevNoneEquatorPct\% at the equator and falls with latitude; only the
stations at \MinElevQualLatLo--\MinElevQualLatHi$^\circ$ keep
\RuleBaselineAvailPct\% baseline availability (\MinElevQualStations\ of

(\MinElevWorstLat$^\circ$) has a satellite above the mask all but

\StationMinElevDeg$^\circ$ mask, so the no-diversity metrics are unchanged.

\emph{Asymmetry.} OneWeb orbits at \OnewebAltKm~km and Starlink at
\StarlinkAltKm~km. As victim, OneWeb receives the PFD-limited signal of the lower,
denser Starlink shell; as interferer, OneWeb's longer slant range and lower EIRP
density weaken its contribution. The \S\,25.261(c) burden therefore falls on the
higher-altitude, lower-EIRP system, which is also the one \S\,25.261(d) protects.

\section{Limitations}
(a) The S.1528 sidelobe of non-serving interferers assumes nadir-pointing beams.
(b) Consumer terminals are flat panels; the S.1428 dish pattern approximates them.
(c) Each station uses its highest-elevation satellite. (d) Diversity is unlimited
and assumes knowledge of the interfering serving satellite. (e) Only the user
downlink is modelled. (f) The OneWeb EIRP is taken from~\cite{delportillo2019};
its constant-EIRP steering and the \StationMinElevDeg$^\circ$ minimum elevation
are assumptions; the \OnewebMinElevSensDeg$^\circ$ result uses the as-deployed
geometry. (g) The primary Starlink power is the regulatory PFD
maximum; Table~\ref{tab:cases} bounds lower power. (h) The as-deployed geometry
is a single snapshot.

\section{Conclusion}
For the Starlink--OneWeb Ku user downlink, the \S\,25.261 $\Delta T/T$ event is
frequent, peaking at \BindDepWorstPct$\pm$\BindDepWorstCi\% of the time for the
OneWeb victim as deployed, \BurdenRatioDep$\times$ the reverse direction. Without
satellite diversity, \S\,25.261(d) is exceeded at Starlink's Gen2 filed maximum
and \PfdOffsetThreeDb~dB below it, but not at the Gen1 design PFD, where the
throughput limit is met and the availability change is not distinguishable from
its limit. Satellite diversity restores compliance only when the victim can hand
off to lower-elevation satellites: with a \StationMinElevDeg$^\circ$ mask both
limits are met in every case, but under OneWeb's filed
\OnewebMinElevSensDeg$^\circ$ design elevation the diversity pool is too small
and the availability limit is exceeded even with diversity. Compliance is
therefore conditional on satellite diversity and on the victim's elevation mask,
neither of which the rule mentions, and the burden falls on the higher-altitude,
lower-EIRP system.
